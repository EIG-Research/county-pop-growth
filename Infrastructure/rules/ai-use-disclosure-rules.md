# AI Use and Disclosure Rules

This file holds the EIG-wide policy on AI in work product, as it applies to every agent and
workflow in this template. It sets what AI may draft, what it may not, where feedback ends and
drafting begins, and how AI use is recorded and disclosed. The policy is EIG's, so a project
cannot relax it in `PROJECT.md`.

## Scope

Work product is anything presented to internal or external readers as EIG work: an analysis,
research report, policy brief, fact sheet, data explainer, blog post, newsletter item, social
post, press release, presentation, or comment letter, with its figures and tables. The
publication cover sheet counts, because internal reviewers read it.

## What AI may and may not do

| AI may draft (disclose it) | AI may not draft, in whole or in part |
|---|---|
| Analysis code, data cleaning, and figure and table code | Any sentence of a work product's prose |
| Images, when disclosed as generated ("Generated by [tool].") | |
| Chart titles, subtitles, annotations, and slide text (owner decision, 2026-10-01) | |
| Data and text analysis | Ledes, headlines, abstracts, thesis statements, and `Why it matters` lines |
| Session logs, plans, specs, review reports, and summary reports for the researcher | Literature-review text meant for a publication |
| Data documentation, codebook notes, and the AI-use ledger | Replacement paragraphs, rewrites, or "tightened" versions of a draft |

AI is not an author on an EIG analysis, blog post, report, or other product. The reason is
that the author answers for every claim and every word under EIG's name, and AI text can be
inaccurate, fabricated, or copied from a source without credit.

## Feedback, not drafting

AI may give feedback on a person's writing. Feedback is:

- naming a problem and where it is (a quote, or the heading and paragraph);
- citing the rule it breaks (for example `Infrastructure/style/docs/eig-writing-style.md` §1);
- asking a question the author should answer (what is the finding, who is the reader);
- describing a change in words ("lead with the finding in paragraph 3", "cut this sentence,
  it restates the one before"); and
- suggesting a swap of a word or short phrase, at most a few words, such as "utilize" to "use"
  or "in order to" to "to".

Feedback never composes a new sentence, clause, lede, headline, or paragraph, even as an
example. The author makes every change in the document. When a fix needs new wording, describe
what the wording must do and leave the writing to the author.

## When asked to draft prose

An agent asked to draft, rewrite, or tighten work-product prose declines, names this rule, and
offers feedback instead (the `eig-writer` coach, the `review-style` workflow, or the
`smart-brevity` critique). A delegated run reports the request to its caller as a blocker.
Code, analysis, and records stay in scope as usual.

## Recording AI use

Every session that uses AI for work product appends an entry to the project's ledger,
`records/ai-use-log.md`, in the shape of `Infrastructure/templates/ai-use-disclosure.md`:
date, tool and model, category, what it did, the outputs it touched, and who reviewed them.
Write the entry before ending or handing off, because the disclosure statement is built from
the ledger and a missing entry becomes a missing disclosure.

Categories: code, data or text analysis, editing feedback, figure or table code, images, and
other. Feedback on a draft counts and is logged as editing feedback.

## Disclosure

Before a work product is circulated or released, the author:

1. tells their supervisor which AI tools were used and how, from the ledger;
2. adds a disclosure statement to the product naming the tool and how it was used, one line per
   use, such as:
   - Editing: "Editing this report was assisted by Claude. All content has been vetted,
     revised, and finalized to ensure accuracy."
   - Analysis: "Claude assisted in analyzing the historical immigration data referenced in
     this publication."
   - Code: "Claude was used to draft initial Python scripts. All code was subsequently tested
     and verified by EIG."
   - Images: "Generated by ChatGPT."

   These are examples. Any other AI-generated content is disclosed too; and
3. records the statement in the cover sheet's `AI Use Disclosure` field
   (`Infrastructure/style/docs/eig-document-process.md` §1).

An agent may write the disclosure statement from the ledger, because it is a record of AI use
and not authored analysis; the author confirms it before it goes into the product (owner
decision, 2026-10-01).

## Author responsibility

The author is responsible for any content they produce or publish that includes AI-generated
material. Before release, they review every AI-assisted part (writing changes taken from AI feedback,
code, data, numbers, claims, citations, images) for accuracy, fabrication, and plagiarism. The review lenses help: `review-ai` checks
citations and provenance, `review-numbers` checks figures against the analysis. They do not
replace the author's review.

## Enforcement

No script can tell whether a person or an AI wrote a sentence, so "AI does not draft prose" is
expected practice, carried by this rule, the agent and workflow bodies, and review, not by a
check. The `eig-writer` coach and `eig-reviewer` agents are currently granted only read tools
in their frontmatter; no check keeps them that way, and a workflow's tool list does not remove
tools from the session that runs it.
