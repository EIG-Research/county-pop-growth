# Idea-Refine Frameworks

Use these lenses selectively during Phase 1. Choose two to four lenses that expose
meaningfully different directions. Do not run every lens or preserve its label when the
label adds no value.

For every direction, keep the unresolved evidence gap distinct from the action proposed
to reduce it. "Run a backtest" is a check; the unknown alert rate or signal stability is
the evidence gap.

## Cross-Mode Lenses

### Reframe the problem

- Move from the named artifact to the job it must perform.
- Restate the tension as: "How might we achieve [outcome] for [audience] while
  respecting [constraint]?"
- Reject a framing that embeds the proposed solution in the problem statement.

### Invert

- Ask what changes if the presumed mechanism, sequence, ownership, or delivery channel
  is reversed.
- Use inversion to expose assumptions, not to manufacture a novelty claim.

### Simplify to the riskiest premise

- Remove components until only the smallest test of the central bet remains.
- Prefer a discriminating test over a miniature version of every eventual feature.

### Change a binding constraint

- Hold the outcome fixed while relaxing or tightening one real constraint: time,
  budget, precision, data access, technical complexity, publication format, or adoption
  burden.
- Explain whether the resulting direction is realistic or only diagnostic.

### Use an analogy carefully

- Borrow a structural solution from another context, not a surface label.
- State which structural similarity must hold and which differences could invalidate
  the analogy.

### Pre-mortem

- Assume the direction produced no useful result.
- Identify the most plausible reason, the earliest warning signal, and whether the
  failure is preventable or disqualifying.

## Research-Question Mode

Create alternatives by changing consequential research choices rather than wording.

### Inferential-goal shift

- Compare a descriptive mapping, decomposition, forecasting, or causal-estimation
  direction.
- Match the claim to what the available design could support. Do not automatically
  privilege causal language.

### Estimand or mechanism shift

- Change the outcome, treatment or exposure, comparison, margin, or mechanism.
- State how the interpretation and data needs change.

### Population, place, or time shift

- Test whether a national average, subgroup contrast, geographic comparison, cohort,
  or episode-specific design better serves the policy question.
- Treat sample size, external validity, and measurement consistency as tradeoffs.

### Measurement-first direction

- Make the first contribution a validated measure, harmonized series, decomposition,
  or descriptive benchmark when weak measurement would undermine every later design.

### Identification-design shift

- Compare credible sources of variation rather than attaching different estimators to
  the same weak comparison.
- Include a transparent descriptive fallback when causal identification is not yet
  defensible.

### Smallest informative analysis

- Identify the table, plot, balance check, first stage, event-study diagnostic, or
  measurement audit that would most quickly reveal whether the project is viable.

## Policy or Communications Product Mode

### Audience-use shift

- Compare products designed for decision support, public explanation, press uptake,
  stakeholder education, or internal alignment.
- Hold the evidence fixed and change how the audience must use it.

### Evidence-layer shift

- Compare a focused empirical finding, synthesis, explanatory framework, or monitored
  indicator.
- Do not promise an evidentiary contribution the source material cannot support.

### Format and cadence shift

- Compare a one-time memo, recurring monitor, brief, interactive, static article, or
  presentation only when format changes use, production cost, or maintenance.

### Minimum useful product

- Identify the smallest publishable or internally usable artifact that answers the
  audience's central question without stripping essential caveats.

## Analytical Tool or Code-Feature Mode

### Workflow-location shift

- Compare preventing an error upstream, detecting it during execution, or auditing it
  downstream.

### Automation-boundary shift

- Compare a fully automated check, human-in-the-loop aid, reusable template, or
  documented manual protocol.
- Treat false assurance and maintenance as costs.

### Integration shift

- Compare extending an existing canonical workflow, adding a narrow helper, or
  introducing a new standalone capability.
- Prefer the smallest reversible seam that avoids duplicate sources of truth.

### Failure-safe version

- Ask how the feature should behave when inputs, dependencies, or context are missing.
- Make blocking, warning, fallback, and recovery behavior part of the direction.

## Workflow or Process Mode

### Bottleneck shift

- Distinguish an input problem, decision problem, execution problem, handoff problem,
  and accountability problem.
- Generate directions aimed at different bottlenecks rather than different meeting
  formats.

### Control-point shift

- Compare a rule, checklist, default, review gate, owner, template, or feedback loop.
- Name who bears the added burden.

### Reversibility shift

- Compare a bounded pilot, opt-in convention, default-on change, and binding policy.
- Prefer a pilot when the behavioral response is the largest unknown.

### Observable-outcome shift

- Define success through reduced rework, shorter cycle time, fewer errors, higher
  adoption, clearer decisions, or another observable result — not mere compliance.
